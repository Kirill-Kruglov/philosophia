I study how capable agents can be improved, evaluated, and governed without having to trust their intentions — or the metrics that reward them.

The work has followed a continuous research trajectory:

From **justitia**, which asks how consequence-responsive constraints can keep evolving agent ecosystems viable, to **proxylimen** and **philosophia**, which investigate whether a learner can derive structure and experience through direct contact rather than inherit them from text.

That trajectory also produced **fallacy-cutter**: a fail-closed experimental instrument designed to make post-hoc thresholds, leakage, missing provenance, and unsupported claims mechanically harder to cite.

My current line, **crucible**, brings the same principle to self-improving LLM agents: a change is not an improvement merely because reward increases. It must also survive held-out evaluation, reproducibility checks, and integrity constraints.

I publish the whole path — working mechanisms, mapped boundaries, preregistered kills, and questions that remain open — as reproducible open research.

• verifiable experimental methodology
• learning through interaction and synthetic worlds
• alignment, governance, and multi-agent systems

Especially interested in collaborations that can turn these research prototypes into joint papers, stronger benchmarks, adversarial audits, or independent replications.
