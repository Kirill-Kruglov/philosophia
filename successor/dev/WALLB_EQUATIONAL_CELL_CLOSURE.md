## Terminal reading

The frame estimate is `2/40 = 0.050`, with a two-sided 95% Wilson interval
`[0.014,0.165]`, for the joint presentation-and-panel generator used by audit
13. The two qualified presentations show sharp conditional signals, but they do
not supply the five presentation blocks required for a multi-world experiment.

Across audit 12d and audit 13, three of forty-eight sampled presentations showed
a strong library-usefulness signal. `3/48` is descriptive only: the audits used
different fixed qualification routing, so it is not a pooled inferential
estimate.

The equational Wall-B cell is therefore closed as too sparse. The threshold is
not relaxed after outcome, the two worlds are not promoted into a new frame,
and no search for predictors of the rare presentations is opened under this
programme.

## What survives

1. **Development method:** before building a scientific experiment around a
   world mechanism, estimate how often that mechanism exists in the declared
   family under a frozen screen.
2. **Bounded development observation:** library usefulness can be strong in
   individual sampled equational presentations, but it was rare under this
   generator and screen.
3. **Programme boundary:** two inexpensive candidate substrates failed to open
   the intended experiment for different reasons: the walk-world invariant was
   trivial, while the equational mechanism was too sparse. This raises the
   estimated price of entering the manufactured-experience question; it does
   not answer that question.
